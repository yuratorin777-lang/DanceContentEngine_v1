# Dance Content Engine — Content Strategy v1

**Status:** Draft for implementation
**Pilot geography:** Серпухов

## 1. Strategic objective

Build a content system that helps parents make better decisions about children's dance, differentiates the school through real expertise and owner knowledge, strengthens trust, and creates a measurable path from attention to inquiry, first lesson, continuation and loyalty.

## 2. Strategic proposition

The content system should not compete primarily through publishing volume or generic dance benefits.

Its differentiation is:

**deep parent decision support + real owner expertise + local relevance + visible development logic.**

## 3. Audience foundation

The current research indicates that parents repeatedly care about:

- child willingness to attend;
- teacher-child relationship;
- visible progress;
- safety and age appropriateness;
- discipline and regularity;
- convenience;
- performance opportunities;
- atmosphere and peer environment;
- communication;
- price/value.

These are recurring qualitative signals, not statistically weighted market percentages.

The working audience model should therefore be built around decision context rather than one demographic persona.

## 4. Primary content objectives

### Objective A — Help choose
Reduce uncertainty before a parent contacts a school.

Topics include age, style, teacher, first lesson, safety, progression and comparison.

### Objective B — Help understand problems
Give parents useful frameworks for difficult situations:

- child does not want to continue;
- adaptation is difficult;
- child is shy;
- progress feels slow;
- parent is unsure whether to push or stop;
- parent is comparing several activities.

### Objective C — Build authority
Use owner knowledge, first-party experience and specific methodology to create content that generic competitors cannot reproduce honestly.

### Objective D — Build relationship
Show the people, philosophy, thinking and standards behind the school.

### Objective E — Create local relevance
Use Serpukhov-specific information when it genuinely helps the audience.

### Objective F — Convert and retain
Support ready-to-act parents and reinforce value for existing families.

## 5. Content mix

The mix should be dynamic rather than fixed by rigid percentages. The initial strategic balance is:

### 1. Decision-support content — core
The largest strategic category.

Examples:
- how to choose dance for a child;
- what age to start;
- how to choose a style;
- how to evaluate a teacher;
- how to recognize progress;
- what happens at the first lesson.

### 2. Problem-solving content — core
Examples:
- child says “I don't want to”;
- adaptation problems;
- fear of stage;
- what to do when progress feels slow;
- how parents can support practice without pressure.

### 3. Authorial / expert content — core differentiation
This is where the new 01_KNOWLEDGE material becomes strategically important.

Examples:
- why the owner stayed in dance for decades;
- what the owner learned about children and parents;
- what became better in dance;
- what became worse;
- why the school's methodology is built the way it is;
- strong positions that challenge common assumptions.

### 4. Proof / progress content
Show actual, verifiable change and evidence.

Use confirmed facts only.

### 5. Local content
Use Serpukhov as real context, not as a keyword insertion.

### 6. Commercial content
Explain offers, groups and next steps only with verified current data.

### 7. Radar-driven content
Use timely signals selectively when they create a meaningful content opportunity.

## 6. Authorial content as a strategic moat

Owner knowledge should not be diluted into generic marketing language.

The content system should preserve:

- personal stories;
- distinctive observations;
- opinions;
- methodology;
- lessons learned;
- contradictions;
- examples;
- long-term experience.

The goal is not to imitate the owner's voice mechanically. The goal is to let real first-party knowledge create differentiated substance.

## 7. Content by decision stage

### Trigger / Awareness
Goal: create recognition and relevance.

Formats:
- short videos;
- provocative author posts;
- observations;
- local signals;
- concise educational ideas.

### Exploration
Goal: answer basic questions.

Formats:
- explainers;
- age guides;
- style guides;
- FAQs;
- parent checklists.

### Shortlist / Comparison
Goal: reduce decision uncertainty.

Formats:
- comparison articles;
- criteria guides;
- teacher-selection guidance;
- what to expect from different formats.

### First Contact / First Lesson
Goal: reduce anxiety and friction.

Formats:
- first-lesson explainers;
- preparation guides;
- adaptation content;
- logistics FAQs.

### Adaptation
Goal: support continuation and correct interpretation of difficulties.

Formats:
- problem-solving posts;
- parent guidance;
- case-based explanations;
- owner perspective.

### Progress / Loyalty
Goal: reinforce value and long-term relationship.

Formats:
- progress stories;
- methodology explanations;
- performances;
- milestones;
- behind-the-scenes expertise.

## 8. Content principles

1. Specific beats generic.
2. Evidence beats slogans.
3. Real owner knowledge beats invented brand personality.
4. Decision support beats repetitive promotion.
5. Local usefulness beats city-name stuffing.
6. A useful post is allowed to sell indirectly.
7. Commercial claims require verification.
8. Radar is a signal source, not a truth authority.
9. Content should vary in angle even when the topic repeats.
10. Strategy should be updated from accumulated analytics, not isolated feelings.

## 9. Topic architecture

The initial topic system should be organized around:

**AGE × PARENT GOAL × CHILD GOAL × DECISION STAGE × FEAR/OBJECTION × STYLE × EVIDENCE.**

This structure is more useful than a flat list of rubrics.

## 10. Radar role

Radar should contribute:

- timely external facts;
- local events;
- research signals;
- cultural context;
- trends;
- external questions or developments that can inspire useful content.

Radar should not:

- decide content priorities by itself;
- replace the owner's knowledge;
- establish unsupported claims;
- force daily news content.

## 11. Future first-party expansion

The strategy should become increasingly informed by:

- CRM questions;
- calls;
- chats;
- reasons for choosing;
- reasons for rejection;
- reasons for dropping;
- analytics performance;
- parent interviews.

These signals will eventually be more valuable than generic external personas for operational content planning.

## 12. What we deliberately do not optimize yet

- maximum posting frequency;
- mass keyword-page creation;
- rigid content percentages;
- platform expansion for its own sake;
- AI-generated trend chasing;
- repeated Writer prompt tuning without performance data.

## 13. Success criteria

Content Strategy v1 is successful when it produces a repeatable mix of useful, differentiated and measurable content across channels and when future analytics can show which decision stages and content jobs generate actual value.
