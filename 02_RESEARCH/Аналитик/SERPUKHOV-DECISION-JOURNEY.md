# SERPUKHOV-DECISION-JOURNEY

**Research cycle:** Audience & Decision Journey Research v2  
**Research date:** 16.08.2026  
**Geography:** Серпухов

## 0. Purpose

This document models how a parent may move from a trigger to enrolment, adaptation, continuation, dropout or switching. It is an evidence model, not a sales funnel and not a Content Strategy.

---

# 1. Core model

**TRIGGER → PROBLEM AWARENESS → EXPLORATION → SHORTLIST → COMPARISON → FIRST CONTACT → FIRST LESSON → ADAPTATION → CONTINUE / DROP → PROGRESS → LOYALTY / SWITCHING**

Not every family passes through every stage linearly. Returning/switching families may start at comparison. Style-led families may enter at exploration. Seasonal enrolment can compress the search cycle.

---

# 2. Stage-by-stage journey

## Stage 1 — TRIGGER

### Parent question
«Почему сейчас нужно/хочется найти ребёнку занятие?»

### Potential triggers
- child asks for activity/style;
- parent decides to add a regular activity;
- previous activity ends;
- school/age transition;
- move;
- recommendation;
- performance interest;
- seasonal enrolment.

### Evidence
Local example of switching after moving to Serpukhov: family already had dance experience and changed studio. Local enrollment windows for additional education demonstrate seasonal decision contexts. citeturn1search6turn0search1

### HYPOTHESIS
Trigger type probably changes the rest of the journey. A child-led trigger may require less persuasion but more style/teacher fit; parent-led trigger may require more explanation of value.

### First-party need
CRM field: «Что произошло/почему начали искать именно сейчас?»

---

## Stage 2 — PROBLEM AWARENESS

### Parent wants to understand
What exactly needs to change: activity, development, socialisation, discipline, interest, physical load, or replacement of another activity.

### Child-side question
«Мне это вообще интересно?»

### Fear
Choosing an activity that solves the parent's problem but does not work for the child.

### Evidence
External research treats enjoyment, perceived competence and social environment as important to persistence. citeturn2search0turn2search7

### UNKNOWN
Actual Serpukhov distribution of parent-led vs child-led triggers.

---

## Stage 3 — EXPLORATION

### Parent questions
- «Какие направления есть?»
- «С какого возраста?»
- «Подойдёт ли моему ребёнку?»
- «Нужны ли способности?»
- «Как вообще проходят занятия?»

### Information need
Clear explanation of age fit, style, process, expected load, teacher model and outcomes.

### Evidence
Local providers structure offers by age/style; the first baseline already documented this market structure.

### INTERPRETATION
At this stage the parent is reducing uncertainty, not yet comparing individual schools.

---

## Stage 4 — SHORTLIST

### Parent questions
- «Куда реально записаться рядом?»
- «Кто работает с нашим возрастом?»
- «Какие варианты есть?»
- «Какие отзывы?»

### Criteria likely used
Location, schedule, age group, style, teacher signals, reviews, price/value.

### Evidence
2GIS currently surfaces 27 dance-training places in Serpukhov, and local reviews contain explicit convenience/teacher/child-enjoyment signals. citeturn0search0turn1search5

### UNKNOWN
No local evidence permits ranking which filter is applied first.

---

## Stage 5 — COMPARISON

### Parent questions
- «Чем одна школа отличается от другой?»
- «Где лучше педагог?»
- «Где ребёнку будет комфортно, но при этом будет развитие?»
- «Есть ли выступления/соревнования?»
- «Какой уровень нагрузки?»

### Child questions
- «Где мне больше нравится?»
- «С кем я буду заниматься?»
- «Какой стиль хочу?»

### Evidence
Local market contains materially different models: sports-dance, modern, choreography/ballet and cultural ensemble; local reviews value teacher fit, enjoyment and performance. citeturn1search5turn1search6

### INTERPRETATION
Comparison is not only provider-vs-provider. It may be **activity-vs-activity** and **training philosophy-vs-training philosophy**.

---

## Stage 6 — FIRST CONTACT

### Parent wants
- quick confirmation of age/group;
- schedule/location;
- price/payment information;
- teacher/group details;
- what to bring;
- what happens at first lesson;
- confidence that administrator will respond clearly.

### Evidence
Local reviews praise availability of management/administration and organisational convenience. citeturn1search5turn1search6

### Barrier
Unclear answer, slow response, hidden conditions, mismatch between expectation and reality.

### First-party priority
Capture unanswered questions and objection immediately before enrolment.

---

## Stage 7 — FIRST LESSON

### Parent evaluates
- child reaction;
- teacher interaction;
- safety/organisation;
- group atmosphere;
- whether the lesson matches promise;
- whether the level seems appropriate.

### Child evaluates
- «Мне нравится?»
- «Я понимаю?»
- «У меня получается?»
- «Мне комфортно с педагогом/детьми?»

### Evidence
Local reviews explicitly use child reaction and teacher approach as post-choice evidence. citeturn1search5turn1search6

### UNKNOWN
No local data establishes the conversion rate from first lesson to ongoing enrolment.

---

## Stage 8 — ADAPTATION

### Parent questions
- «Нормально ли, что ребёнок не хочет идти?»
- «Нормально ли, что сначала не получается?»
- «Сколько может длиться привыкание?»
- «Когда вмешиваться?»

### Child-side signals
- reluctance;
- anxiety;
- fatigue;
- confusion;
- gradual competence;
- attachment to teacher/group;
- enjoyment.

### Evidence
Local review of Сильфида documents a previous period where the child needed persuasion and later strongly wanted to attend after switching; this shows adaptation/fit can change, but one case cannot establish a general adaptation curve. citeturn1search6

External youth-sport literature indicates that enjoyment, competence, coach relationship, peer acceptance and parental support relate to persistence, while pressure can contribute to dropout. citeturn2search0turn2search1

### Owner evidence
Owner explicitly observes many children leaving after a first «не хочу» and believes some difficulties are a necessary part of learning. This is owner observation, not population-level proof.

### IMPORTANT ANALYTICAL GUARDRAIL
The evidence does **not** justify a universal rule «родитель должен заставлять». A more defensible model is: distinguish temporary adaptation difficulty from persistent negative experience, while considering child autonomy, enjoyment, competence and relationship quality.

### First-party priority
Need longitudinal notes at week 1 / month 1 / month 3 and reason codes for continuation/dropout.

---

## Stage 9 — CONTINUE / DROP

### Continue signals
- child wants to attend;
- perceived progress;
- teacher relationship;
- peer belonging;
- parent sees value;
- logistics remain manageable.

### Drop signals
- persistent lack of enjoyment;
- perceived inability/no progress;
- conflict with teacher/group;
- school/time conflicts;
- injury/health issue;
- cost/logistics;
- competing activity.

External systematic review identifies lack of enjoyment, competence, social pressure, competing priorities and physical factors as major dropout areas. citeturn2search7

A 2025 study on parental role in youth-sport dropout also identifies parental stress, conflicts, injuries, other hobbies, time constraints and communication gaps as relevant contextual factors. citeturn2search4

### UNKNOWN for Serpukhov
No reliable local dropout proportions or ranked reasons.

---

## Stage 10 — PROGRESS

### Parent questions
- «Есть ли результат?»
- «Что ребёнок уже умеет?»
- «Что изменилось?»
- «Куда можно расти дальше?»

### Child question
«У меня получается лучше, чем раньше?»

### Evidence
Local reviews use progress/skills and performance as positive signals; external persistence literature identifies perceived competence, development and positive feedback as protective factors. citeturn1search5turn2search6

### INTERPRETATION
Progress must be legible to both actors: parent needs observable evidence; child needs felt competence.

---

## Stage 11 — LOYALTY / SWITCHING

### Loyalty signals
- strong teacher attachment;
- peer group;
- child identity around activity;
- visible progress;
- performance/competition goals;
- organisational trust.

### Switching triggers
- relocation;
- persistent mismatch with teacher/group;
- schedule conflict;
- perceived stagnation;
- changing child interests;
- competing priorities.

### Evidence
Local review documents switching after relocation; external soccer research found post-dropout behaviour can differ between switching provider and leaving the activity depending on attributed causes. citeturn1search6turn2search5

### UNKNOWN
No local switching-rate data.

# 3. Segment differences across the journey

| Stage | Preschool / first-time | Development/hobby | Performance/progression | Re-entry/switching |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trigger | parent/child initiation, age stage | need for useful activity | ambition/result/performance | dissatisfaction, move, changed needs |
| Exploration | safety, adaptation, age fit | value, schedule, enjoyment | training model, progression | diagnosis of previous mismatch |
| Comparison | teacher, group, logistics | atmosphere/value | results, level, teacher, competition | reasons for switching |
| First lesson | emotional safety + approach | interest + comfort | level + technical fit | proof that previous problem is solved |
| Adaptation | high uncertainty | moderate | potentially demanding | expectation of better fit |
| Continue | child wants to return | perceived value + enjoyment | progress + goals | restored trust + better fit |
| Drop | adaptation/fit uncertainty | boredom/competing priorities | overload/stagnation/goal conflict | repeat of prior problem |

**Status:** working model; only parts backed by direct local evidence. Segment-specific differences beyond observed cases are hypotheses.

# 4. Continue / Drop / Switching: evidence synthesis

## Confirmed externally
Enjoyment, perceived competence, coach relationship, peer acceptance, parental support, competing priorities and structural constraints all appear in youth sport persistence/dropout literature. citeturn2search0turn2search7turn2search12

## Confirmed locally only as examples
- child initially needed persuasion and later became enthusiastic after changing studio;
- child enjoyment and teacher approach are repeatedly mentioned;
- convenience/location appears in positive review evidence. citeturn1search5turn1search6

## UNKNOWN
- what proportion of local dropouts are due to child motivation vs parent decision;
- whether «не хочу» is temporary adaptation or persistent mismatch;
- exact switching reasons;
- time-to-dropout distribution;
- whether price is a primary or secondary cause locally.

# 6. First-party measurement points

The most valuable data should be captured at transition points:

**Trigger → enquiry**: trigger language.  
**Enquiry → first lesson**: objection, source, missing information.  
**First lesson → continuation**: child reaction + parent decision.  
**Month 1/3 → retention**: motivation, progress, teacher fit, logistics.  
**Dropout → exit**: exact reason in parent words.  
**Switching → new choice**: why previous provider failed and what new provider promised.

---

# 7. Decision Journey conclusion

The journey is best understood as a sequence of **uncertainty reductions**:

1. Why do we need an activity?
2. Which type fits the child?
3. Which providers are credible and practical?
4. Which one is the best fit?
5. Did the first real experience match the expectation?
6. Is difficulty normal adaptation or evidence of mismatch?
7. Is the child progressing and willing to continue?
8. If not, what exactly failed: child, teacher, group, schedule, cost, goal or environment?

This model is sufficiently structured for downstream strategic work, but the missing first-party measurements must not be filled with invented weights.
